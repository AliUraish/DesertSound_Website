Slow Wi-Fi is usually placement, an old router, or too many devices on one box — not "the internet."

### Check the current setup

Put the router central and high, not behind a TV unit. Age matters: an old router may not keep up with today's phones, TVs, cameras, and consoles. If the device list is long, the box needs to handle that load.

### Upgrade the hardware

Dual-band splits traffic across 2.4 GHz (range, walls) and 5 GHz (speed, shorter reach). A second 5 GHz band on a tri-band unit helps when the house is crowded.

A [mesh Wi-Fi system](/service/home-networking-and-wi-fi/) covers large or multi-floor homes without dead rooms. Wi-Fi 6 is the current standard to look for on a new router.

### Tune what you already have

Switch to a quieter channel if neighbours crowd yours. Password-protect the network — WPA3 if available. Install firmware updates. An open or default-password network shares your bandwidth with whoever is nearby.

### Extend coverage without guessing

Powerline adapters use electrical wiring. They can help in a finished home; results depend on the circuit. Ethernet to the TV, console, or desk still beats another wireless hop and frees airtime for phones.
