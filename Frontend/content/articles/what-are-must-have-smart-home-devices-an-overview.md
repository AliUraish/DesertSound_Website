A [smart home system](/service/smart-home-automation/) is a set of devices on one network you can run from a phone, keypad, or voice. Almost anything can be "smart." Most of it isn't worth installing.

Start with what you'll use every day. Then pick a platform — Alexa, Google, or a dedicated hub — and only add devices that talk to it.

### What actually belongs in the house

- Lights and switches
- Climate control
- Speakers and streaming
- Locks, doorbells, and cameras
- Smoke or CO detectors that alert your phone

Outlets, garage controls, and appliances are optional. A smart coffee maker is a gadget. Lighting and a lock you can check from outside are infrastructure.

### How the devices talk

Most of them use Wi-Fi. Battery sensors often use Zigbee or Z-Wave and need a hub. Wi-Fi is fine for a small set of always-powered devices. A house full of battery sensors on Wi-Fi drains batteries and crowds the router.

Check the radio and the hub before you buy. A lock that won't join the system you already own is just a lock.

### Do you need a hub?

Not always. Some speakers already act as one. Many lights and sensors still need a separate hub. If a device you own can run the network, don't buy a second brain.

### Mix and match

More brands play together than they used to. Daily use is still simpler if you pick one assistant and stay with it. Start with lighting or security, then expand.
