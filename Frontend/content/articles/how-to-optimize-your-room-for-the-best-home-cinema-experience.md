The room decides more than the TV. A high-end display in a bright, echoey lounge still looks and sounds ordinary.

### Pick the room

Quieter, less traffic, light you can control. A spare room beats a glass-walled lounge if you want cinema. You can still treat a living room; you just work harder on shades and reflections.

### Seating

Face the screen. Distance about 1.5 to 2.5 times the screen's diagonal. A second row needs risers or a larger image, or the back row watches a letterbox.

### Acoustics

Rugs, curtains, and upholstery cut slap-back. Panels at first-reflection points. Soundproofing is a separate job if you share walls — panels don't stop bass leaving the room.

### Screen or TV

Projector: size the screen to the room, not the biggest number in the catalogue. Neutral-gain screens are the usual start. TV: eye height when seated. An adjustable mount helps more than a fixed tilt you regret.

Use HDMI that matches the resolution. A streamer or disc player that isn't the TV's built-in app if you care about picture.

### Sound

Place speakers before you buy more of them. Fronts at ear height, surrounds beside or behind, sub where it actually sounds even — then calibrate levels and delay.
