Home theatre service is the design, install, calibration, and support — not dropping a TV on a stand.

### Installation

Speaker placement, screen alignment, and cable routes decide whether the room works. Miss those and even good gear sounds thin.

A novice install usually gets the TV on the wall and the soundbar under it, then stops. Surrounds end up on the floor. The sub goes in a corner because that is where it fit. That is how you get boom and no dialogue.

A professional installer knows where the surrounds belong relative to the seat, how high the screen should sit, and which cables need to be in the wall before the painter arrives.

### Calibration

Out-of-box picture modes are too bright and too blue. Audio defaults ignore your seat. We set levels, delay, and picture to the room.

Calibration is not a vibe. Every speaker should arrive at the main seat at the same time, at a balanced level. The picture should hold detail in dark scenes without glowing like a shop display.

### Cable Management

Hidden runs look better and last longer. Floor cables get kicked and fail. Trunking, in-wall, or a proper raceway also keeps HDMI bends sane so you are not fighting a flaky picture six months later.

### What the Job Looks Like

Consultation: the room, seating, how you watch, what stays. Install: mount, wire, hide, connect. Calibration: measure, not guess. Handover: how to use it, and who to call when something drifts.

Expect questions about windows, kids, gaming, and whether the room is shared. Those answers change the design.

### Support

Settings get reset. HDMI handshake fails. A local installer who already knows the rack is faster than a generic repair shop. Periodic checks catch a dying HDMI lead or a receiver that has been shoved against a closed cabinet.


### Why People Call After a DIY Weekend

The picture is fine. The sound is not. Or the sound is loud and the picture is washed out. Or everything works until someone switches to the console.

Those are service problems: placement, calibration, and control. Buying another box rarely fixes them. A site visit usually does — sometimes without new gear.

We also flag rooms that cannot take a projector yet: too much glass, no blackout, no throw. Better to hear that before the unit is on a shelf.

Maintenance is the other half. A yearly check of HDMI, filters, and firmware is cheaper than a “dead” system that only needed a lead and a picture mode reset.
