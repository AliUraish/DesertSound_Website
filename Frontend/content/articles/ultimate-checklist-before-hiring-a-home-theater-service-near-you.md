A cheap quote that leaves cables flapping and dialogue missing is not a bargain. Use this list before you hire anyone.

### Do they inspect the room or just sell gear?

A serious installer asks about room size, windows, seating, and power — including how often it drops. "We'll see on the day" is a stall. They should tell you if a projector will wash out in a bright lounge, or if a large screen will dominate a small apartment, before they unload boxes.

### Ask for real installs, not a showroom

Request photos or short video of finished rooms like yours. Showroom shots and stock images do not count. Real work shows concealed wiring, the finished seating line, and before/after of the same space.

### What the price actually includes

Get the quote in writing. Ask whether cable concealment, power distribution, and audio plus video calibration are in the number. If they say "installation" but will not commit to those, assume they are extras.

### Can they explain placement without a sales script?

They should say where the centre speaker goes, why the subwoofer location matters, and whether your [smart home automation](/service/smart-home-automation/) or [control system](/service/control-systems/) needs to change. "Dolby Atmos" with no explanation of a bare floor, rugs, or panels is a red flag.

### Reviews, warranty, and follow-up

Read reviews and notice repeated complaints — especially no follow-up. Ask for the warranty on the install, not only the boxes. Get a response window in writing if a cable loosens or the system drifts. "We'll come by sometime" is not support.

### Timeline, power, and later upgrades

Ask how many days, whether work is staged, and how furniture and floors are protected. Ask about a dedicated circuit, ventilation for the projector and receiver, HDMI bandwidth, and ARC/eARC if you use TV apps. Wiring should leave a path to add a source later without ripping the wall again.
