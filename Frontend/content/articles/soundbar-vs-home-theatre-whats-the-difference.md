A soundbar is one enclosure under the TV. A home theatre is speakers around the room, plus a sub. That's the difference that matters — not the marketing names.

TV speakers are thin. Either option beats them. Which one you buy depends on room size, how much wiring you'll accept, and whether you want sound behind you.

### Soundbar

A soundbar packs left, right, and usually a center into one bar. Some add a wireless sub. HDMI or optical from the TV is enough for most setups. Bluetooth covers phones.

Dialogue gets clearer. Bass improves if there's a real sub, not a tiny driver in the bar. You still won't get true surround — the sound comes from the front wall.

### Home theatre

A home theatre uses separate speakers: front left and right, a center for dialogue, surrounds, and a subwoofer. Layouts run from 2.1 up to 5.1, 7.1, or Dolby Atmos with height speakers.

That layout is why films feel like they're around you. It also means cable runs, placement, and calibration. In a Karachi apartment, that only works if the room is planned — not if five boxes are dropped in corners.

### Setup

A soundbar is a short install: power, one cable to the TV, maybe a sub pairing. A theatre needs speaker positions, hidden wiring, and level matching so the center doesn't get buried.

### Which to buy

- **Soundbar:** small lounge, rental, or you don't want to chase cables. Fine for news and casual streaming.
- **Home theatre:** a dedicated room or a large lounge, movies and sports that should sit around you, and a budget for install — not just the boxes.

Measure the room before you shop. Furniture and glass change both options. If you want surround, start with a 5.1 plan, not a bar that claims it.
