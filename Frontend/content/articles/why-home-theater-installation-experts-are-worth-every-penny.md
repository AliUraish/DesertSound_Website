Buying the equipment is the easy part. Making it work in a specific Karachi living room is the job.

A [home theatre install](/service/home-theatre-design-and-installation/) is not unpacking boxes. It's seating distance matched to screen size, speakers aimed at the row, cables hidden before the walls close, and calibration with measurement — not by ear from the doorway.

### What goes wrong without that

Wrong speaker height. No usable center channel, so dialogue sits in the TV. HDMI runs that can't hold 4K. A sub in the corner that booms one note and loses the rest. Those mistakes cost more to undo in a finished room than to do once.

### What you actually pay for

The room is measured. The layout is specific to that space, not a generic 5.1 diagram. The rack is labeled. Extra conduit is left for a later source. [Smart home](/service/smart-home-automation/) and multi-room audio are part of the same plan, so the theatre isn't a second island of remotes.

Electrical work is done to a standard you can live with. That's the safety piece DIY skips.
