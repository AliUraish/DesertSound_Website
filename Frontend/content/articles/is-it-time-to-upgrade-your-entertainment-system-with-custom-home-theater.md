A bigger TV on the same bright wall is not an upgrade. If dialogue is thin, the image washes out, or the room still looks like a cable tangle, the layout is the problem.

### What custom actually means

A custom theatre is a system sized to the room: screen or projector, speakers that match the volume of the space, light control, and seats that see the image without strain. Sports, games, and films all use the same stack if placement is right.

- Surround that keeps effects off the dialogue
- One room that works for more than movies
- A layout that fits the walls you have, not a catalogue plan

### When an upgrade is worth it

Streaming and discs already outrun an old panel and a soundbar in a hard room. A designed install hides the wiring and sets levels so the room stops fighting the soundtrack. A finished cinema can also help a house sell — if it looks like a room, not a shop display.

### The pieces that matter

Screen or projector first, then speakers and a processor that can hold dialogue and bass without one burying the other. 5.1 or 7.1 only helps if the surrounds are actually around the seats. Dimmed lighting or blackout on the screen wall is not decoration; it is contrast.

Plan acoustics before you buy a louder amplifier. Neighbours and bare rooms do not forgive a late fix. Match spend to the room: a soundbar-plus-TV stack is a different job from a projector and a full speaker set.

### Mistakes that waste the upgrade

A screen too large for the seating distance. Picture spend with no audio plan. Cables left on the floor. Skipping a test night before sign-off.

Professionals mount the projector, pull cable, and calibrate so you are not guessing throw and speaker delay. DIY can work for a simple lounge. It rarely works for in-wall cable and a projected image.

Wireless speakers and a single control layer make daily use simpler. Efficiency ratings on displays and amps are worth checking; they are not a substitute for a room that is built correctly.
